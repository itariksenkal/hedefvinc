Working in Bodrum

Bodrum has narrow, steep streets, garden walls and plenty of overhead cables. On most villa jobs the real question is not the weight of the load but where the crane can stand. A location pin and a few photos of the street, the gate and the garden usually tell us whether we need to visit first.

In summer the traffic is heavy; planned jobs are easier early in the morning. If the crane has to stand in a street or on a pavement, let us talk about it before the day.

Boat lifting

Boats are lifted with wide soft straps, never chains or ropes around the hull. The straps must stay clear of the shaft, propeller and transducers; if your hull has sling marks, we use them. The first lift is only a few centimetres, to check the balance before the full lift.

Empty the fuel and water tanks as far as you can, and have the cradle or stands ready if the boat stays ashore. On windy days a lift may be postponed.

On the job

Frequently asked questions

Does the crane come with an operator?
Yes. Our cranes come with our own operator, and planning the lift and choosing the slings is our job. You only need to show us where the load should go.
How far ahead should I book?
For planned jobs, a few days ahead makes it easier to get the day and time you want. In the busy boat season, book earlier. For urgent jobs, call us any time.
Can a crane get into a narrow street?
Often, but not always. The access road, corners, slope and the space for the outriggers decide it. If we are not sure, we come and look first. A location pin and photos of the street usually tell us enough.
What do you need to know to lift my boat?
Length, beam, type (motor or sail) and approximate weight; where the boat is and where it should go; and the ground where the crane will stand. If your hull has sling position marks, we use them.
How is the price worked out?
From the weight and size of the load, the lifting height and reach, how long the job takes, the address and the site conditions. We give you a clear price once we have seen the job.
